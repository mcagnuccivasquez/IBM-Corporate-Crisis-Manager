Appendix B · Risk method

Step 1, per domain. Each domain (reputational, economic, legal/regulatory) is scored for Severity (S) and Likelihood/Exposure (L), each from 1 to 4. Domain score = S × L.

Severity (how bad if it happens / continues)Likelihood / exposure (how probable and widespread now)
1Minor: isolated, single customer, no safety/data dimension, cost < €10kRemote: claim disproved or no plausible mechanism; no exposure
2Moderate: several customers or limited public visibility; cost €10k–100kPossible: single unverified report; limited reach
3Major: safety or personal-data question on a product line in market; broad public attention; cost €100k–1M; regulatory notification possibleLikely: multiple independent reports, or a defect confirmed in samples; affected units in market; reach growing
4Severe: confirmed injury/fire, confirmed access to recordings, authority action, withdrawal across markets; cost > €1MOccurring: harm, media coverage, retailer or authority action already happening

Step 2, overall level. Highest domain score: 1–3 Low · 4–6 Medium · 8–9 High · 12–16 Critical.

Step 3, floors.

  • F1 Credible report of fire, smoke, burning, electric shock or injury involving a product on the market, even if unconfirmed → at least High.
  • F2 Confirmed injury, a hazardous defect confirmed in market units, or a request/contact from an authority → Critical.
  • F3 Confirmed or likely unauthorised access to customer personal data or recordings → at least High.
  • F4 The level may only drop versus the previous version if a claim was disproved or downgraded, or an action was verified effective.

Likelihood is about the world (exposure, spread, mechanism); evidence confidence is about our sources. Both are reported and never merged. A user-set focus changes ordering and depth, never scores or the level.
